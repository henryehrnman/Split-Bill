Product schema as follows:
  1) initial funding and account setup:
    i) Members make accounts and an individual would create a payment group. Within the creation of the payment group, this individual would decide on a budget, which includes a group budget, and the period of time that budget is for (e.g. $300 every 2 weeks). They would then invite other accounts to join their group by typing in their userid, email, or phone number in the app, and selecting the profile that comes up. When accepting an invite to a group, the details of the group are displayed to the user who will then agree and join or disagree. A group fund is created, and each member is issued a visa card that is connected to that account. No member, including the creator, pays while invitations are still being resolved. Once all invitees have accepted or declined and at least two members remain, the group is fully created; the first budget period starts then. Each joined member is then automatically charged their equal share of the final budget. Before creating or accepting an invitation, users see that the current contribution is an estimate, that declines can increase it, and the maximum possible contribution with two members. Each member authorizes the displayed maximum, and the server records it; collection never exceeds that authorization. Older app versions that submit only a reviewed estimate authorize no more than that estimate; legacy joined members without a recorded authorization are not charged additional initial funding. If the final share exceeds a recorded authorization, the remainder stays unpaid and automatic period refills are disabled until the member opts in under the final terms. The final response that activates the group triggers collection; earlier responses do not. A failed initial payment is recorded for that member without undoing other successful contributions or preventing the group from becoming active. Any member can add a card assigned to the group to their e-wallet and pay for group purchases. Each member's individual balance and spend is tracked seperately, but all funds are allocated to the same group fund (outlined in 2, 5.ii).
    ii) After the group becomes active, any member may propose increasing or decreasing its periodic funding target. Every joined member must approve. The new target applies at the next period start, using each member's existing automatic or manual refill choice. Lowering the target leaves existing balances and surplus untouched. A missed or failed contribution does not restrict a member whose settled balance is nonnegative (2.iv).
    iii) At the beginning of each new period, users either manually add funds or are automatically charged the amount required to top them up to their equal split of the budget. (e.g. if you had a group budget of $300 for 3 people, which is a $100 contribution per person, and had $40 in your account at the end of the period, you would only be charged $60 at the beginning of the next period).

  2) purchases:
    i) On purchase, funds are withdrawn from the group fund, and the person who made the purchase sends the first proposal. They can first select who was part of the charge and by how much (or scan the receipt for an itemized split), then send that proposal, and only the people with a share in it are notified. If they leave the app without doing anything, the group's default split (defaults to equally across members, can be changed) is sent automatically and everyone involved is notified. The current pending split is displayed on the main fund screen under the group fund amount. From there, each involved user can modify, propose, and accept the split while other group purchases continue.
    ii) Splits can be itemized, by percent, or by dollar amount. Splits must always cover the entire bill. We do not round or drop cents, and all charge must be allocated before a split is accepted.
    iii) Charge splits are iterative, meaning the first split proposed by a given user is shown to the rest of the group, and then each other user can choose to accept or modify, with the requirement being each user involved in a transaction must agree to the split (if a user is not part of a transaction, say in a case where they did not go to a group dinner, other members can accept a split that does not allocate any spend to that user without needing their approval). 
    iv) Any joined member with a personal balance of $0 or more may make a purchase up to the available group fund, even if they have not added their share for the current period. Their own balance does not cap the purchase amount. The configured budget is a periodic funding target, not a per-purchase ceiling: a $300-budget group with $500 available can make a $400 purchase. Extra deposits and retained surplus remain spendable; only available pooled funds limit the amount. A negative personal balance blocks that member from paying until it is brought back to $0. Group freezes and card controls still apply.
    v) Several purchases may be open at once; there is no one-purchase lock or setting. Every unsettled purchase reserves its full amount from the group fund, so concurrent purchases cannot reuse the same money. Proposed personal shares do not reduce a member's spending limit before settlement; their nonnegative settled balance and the remaining group fund determine whether they can pay.

  3) after a split is accepted:
    i) Once a split has been accepted, each users tracked balance is decreased by the agreed upon amount. Buying power of the card is decreased accordingly. After activation, users may add funds freely. Withdrawals are limited to the member's positive balance and the available group fund, and are blocked while any split is unsettled or the group is frozen.
    ii) If a user's periodic top-up fails or they choose not to add more funds, the group has less available money. That member can still pay from the available group fund while their own balance is nonnegative (outlined in 2.iv).
    iii) If a purchase takes a user's personal balance below 0 once the split is confirmed, their personal card is automatically charged the absolute value of their negative balance to bring it back to 0. If this charge fails, their account is temporarily suspended until the debt is repaid. Importantly, this debt is on the other members of the group, and the group buying power will fall below the other member's balances until rectified. 

  4) disputes:
    i) The product mainly operates on a system of trust, it is expected that members within a group are on a good basis with each other (roommates, spouses, friends, coworkers, etc.), and the product serves to streamline the payment process for group purchases. Any disputes, fraud, or other split issues can not be solely handled by the product itself, and in the result of a major lasting issue, the product keeps the right to resolve the dispute as they see fit, and disband the payment group. If users cannot agree upon a split within a week of a charge, the account is frozen, and the charge is escalated to a dispute center (not implementing in this scope, just as a hypothetical fallback).
    ii) User chargebacks after spend also results in freezing the account, automatically escalating to the dispute center as potential fraud, where support chooses how to handle the member and group.
    iii) There are a multitude of other edge cases and issues that we will choose to ignore here. We're working on an idea & MVP for a hackathon. We want this succesfully built and fleshed out, but it does not need to be an enterprise standard financial system from day one.

  5) features and tracking
    i) At time of purchase, users can choose to upload a picture of the reciept, which the product will scan and store. When a receipt is successfully processed, the itemized bill will be added to the split, and users can choose to add items to their own charge (with the total tax on the receipt defaulting to being allocated per member by amount on items taken responsability for). Items can also be split between users, or edited if the product processed something wrong, and users may still choose to split based on their own dollar amounts or percentages if they wish. For an itemized split, each user will take responsability for items and then lock in their selection. Other users can then see their selections, and choose to suggest an edit to another users items if they wish, or simply choose their own items and continue. Once all items are accounted for and there are no pending edits, the itemized split is confirmed and charged.
    ii) A chargeback freezes the group but does not itself refund or reverse a settled charge; that spend remains in totals until an actual reversal is implemented. Each charge and the agreed upon split will be stored and tracked in a seperate tab, so each user can see the monthly and total spend for the group, along with the charges and their details. Each user can also see their own balance, spend, and charges they were involved in within their own profile at any time.

  6) generative AI (proposal, reviewed against the schema above; nothing here is built yet)
    Ground rule: AI only drafts or explains. It never moves money, never agrees for a member, and never decides who is restricted, suspended, or at fault. Every AI output goes through the same validation and approval rules as a human action (split math in code, every involved member accepts, every claimant locks). Model calls run on the backend, not in the app, so keys and receipt photos never sit on the phone.
    i) Still makes sense:
      a) Receipt scanning (5.i): a vision model reads the photo into line items, tax and tip. The result is checked against the card charge amount; if it doesn't add up, users fix items before claiming. This is the highest-value use and should be the first one built.
      b) Natural-language item claiming (5.i): "I had the burger and half the fries" fills in YOUR selection on the claim board. You still review it and lock it in yourself. AI can never lock, or claim items for someone else.
      c) Suggested claims: prefill your likely items from your past orders at the same merchant (e.g. you always get the oat latte). Only a draft, shown before you lock.
      d) Natural-language percent/amount proposals (2.ii, 2.iii): "Jordan wasn't at dinner, split the rest evenly" becomes a draft proposal. Code computes the exact cents and the normal accept flow applies.
      e) Spending categories and insights (5.ii): label charges (groceries, utilities, dining) and add period-aware insights, e.g. "you've used 70% of your share with 5 days left in this period".
      f) Budget suggestions (1.ii): suggest a budget per period from past periods ("you've averaged $340 every 2 weeks"). Changing it still needs everyone's approval through the normal proposal.
      g) Stuck split help (2.v, 4.i): before the 7-day deadline, summarize where people disagree (competing proposals, pending item edits, who hasn't locked) and send neutral nudges. Helps avoid a freeze; it doesn't decide anything.
      h) Read-only Q&A over group history: "How much have we spent on takeout this month?" or "Why was my bank charged $12?" Answers come from stored data and link to the charges.
    ii) Doesn't make sense:
      a) AI accepting splits, locking item selections, or approving item edits for a member. Each involved person must agree (2.iii, 5.i).
      b) AI resolving disputes or chargebacks, or deciding who is at fault. That goes to the dispute center and support (4.i, 4.ii).
      c) AI proposing or applying budget changes, top-ups, withdrawals, or bank charges on its own. Money movement follows fixed rules and member actions (1.ii, 1.iii, 3.iii).
      d) AI doing split math or rounding. Cents must be exact and fully allocated, so this stays in deterministic code (2.ii).
      e) AI risk scoring or deciding restrictions and suspensions. Those follow the fixed balance rules (negative balance, failed debt recovery) in 2.iv and 3.iii.
      f) Fuzzy AI matching for invites. Users must type an exact user ID, email or phone number and pick the profile themselves (1.i), so the wrong person can't be added to a shared fund.
      g) A chatbot replacing the core screens. Fund, split, card and activity flows stay as direct UI; chat is optional help on top.
