Product schema as follows:
  1) initial funding and account setup:
    i) Members make accounts and join payment groups. Per each group, each member contribues an equal initial deposit, which sets the limit on the buying power for the group (i.e. a 3 member group choosing $300 buying power for the card each initially deposit $100. We setup a card pointing at the group fund per person per group with visa, so any member can add a card assigned to the group to their e-wallet and pay for group purchases. Each member's individual balance and spend is tracked seperately, but all funds are allocated to the same group fund.
    ii) At any time, users can propose changing the buying power of the card, either increasing or decreasing it. Increasing requires each user to deposit enough money to cover their equal split, wheras decreasing keeps balances the same, keeping any surplus, but only requiring balances to be topped up to the new decreased amount.

  2) purchases:
    i) On purchase, funds are withdrawn from the group fund, and the app tentitaviely proposes the set default split for the charge (there would be some menu for current pending charge splits, it would default to whatever the users have chosen as the automatic split (defaults to equally across members, can be changed)), from there, each user goes into the app some time before the group's next purchase, and can modify, propose, and accept the split. 
    ii) Splits must always cover the entire bill. We do not round or drop cents, and all charge must be allocated before a split is accepted.
    iii) Charge splits are iterative, meaning the first split proposed by a given user is shown to the rest of the group, and then each other user can choose to accept or modify, with the requirement being each user involved in a transaction must agree to the split (if a user is not part of a transaction, say in a case where they did not go to a group dinner, other members can accept a split that does not allocate any spend to that user without needing their approval). 
    iv) Users may spend more than their individual buying power, as groups operate on a foundation of trust between members. However, if users are unable to keep their balance topped up to the agreed upon amount (outlined in 3.i-ii), their buying power is temporarily restricted to their own personal balance until they add the required funds.
    v) Each split must be accepted before the next purchase is made, we do not allow for multiple floating charges, as that could allow users to spend greatly over their own capability. Once the split has been accepted and balances updated, the card is ready for another purchase.

  3) after a split is accepted:
    i) Once a split has been accepted, each users tracked balance is decreased by the agreed upon amount, and they have to either choose to manually add balance to their account, or set up autodeposit to always keep their balance (and therefore the group buying power) at the same amount. individual balances can always be adjusted, with users freely able to withdraw or add funds.
    ii) If a user's top-up fails or they choose not to add more funds, their balance reflects that, meaning the group buying power (and their individual spend approval) is reduced to their current balance.

  4) disputes:
    i) The product mainly operates on a system of trust, it is expected that members within a group are on a good basis with each other (roommates, spouses, friends, coworkers, etc.), and the product serves to streamline the payment process for group purchases. Any disputes, fraud, or other split issues can not be solely handled by the product itself, and in the result of a major lasting issue, the product keeps the right to resolve the dispute as they see fit, and disband the payment group. If users cannot agree upon a split within a week of a charge, the account is frozen, and the charge is escalated to a dispute center (not implementing in this scope, just as a hypothetical fallback).
    ii) User chargebacks after spend also results in freezing the account, automatically escalating to the dispute center as potential fraud, where support chooses how to handle the member and group.
    iii) There are a multitude of other edge cases and issues that we will choose to ignore here. We're working on an idea & MVP for a hackathon. We want this succesfully built and fleshed out, but it does not need to be an enterprise standard financial system from day one.

  5) features and tracking
    i) At time of purchase, users can choose to upload a picture of the reciept, which the product will scan and store. When a receipt is successfully processed, the itemized bill will be added to the split, and users can choose to add items to their own charge (with the total tax on the receipt defaulting to being allocated per member by amount on items taken responsability for). Items can also be split between users, or edited if the product processed something wrong, and users may still choose to split based on their own dollar amounts or percentages if they wish. 
    ii) Each charge and the agreed upon split will be stored and tracked in a seperate tab, so each user can see the monthly and total spend for the group, along with the charges and their details. Each user can also see their own balance, spend, and charges they were involved in within their own profile at any time.
