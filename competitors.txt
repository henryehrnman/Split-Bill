Competitors and positioning (research, October 17, 2026; for the hackathon pitch)
  Use: one slide and a 20-second answer to "how is this different from Splitwise / Venmo / Cino?" Don't spend more build time on this before 10am.

  1) the market, in three groups
    i) Pay first, chase later (one person fronts the money, everyone repays):
      a) Splitwise: expense tracker, settle via Splitwise Pay (US), Venmo or PayPal. Huge network, no card.
      b) Venmo Groups (since Nov 2023): add expenses, see who owes what, settle up inside Venmo. No card.
      c) splyt (Splyt Tech Inc.): host pays, uploads the receipt, friends claim items and repay through Venmo/Cash App. About 50,000 monthly users (Google for Startups profile).
      d) Split - Pay Together (Split Mobile Inc., splitapp.co): host scans a receipt and sends a pay link; guests pay by Apple Pay/Google Pay/card without the app. 4.7 stars from 779 ratings; 5% fee on guest card payments per developer replies; reviews complain about scan accuracy, fees and slow payouts. Virtual cards are listed as "not live yet / future roadmap".
      e) tricount: expense tracking, tied to bunq cards in Europe.
    ii) Split at the moment of payment (group card that charges each person's own card):
      a) Cino: shared virtual Mastercard, each share is charged instantly to members' personal cards. Raised EUR 3.5M (Balderton, 2025). EU/EEA + UK only; no US launch found. Splits are set when paying, so they don't adapt to who actually ordered what.
      b) Splita: shared virtual Visa linked to your debit card, "split in real time". Waitlist (~1,200), appears UK-focused.
      c) Split (split-card.com): waitlist; equal/custom splits and AI receipt scanning.
    iii) Pooled money (everyone pays into one pot first):
      a) Splitcard: organizer collects shares into a pool by link; one group virtual card. Organizers adjust splits.
      b) Zeta: joint bank account and cards for couples; either partner can empty or close the account.

  2) what makes Split Bill different
    i) Nobody fronts the money and nobody chases. Unlike group i, every member pays from the shared fund with their own card.
    ii) The split is agreed after the purchase, by the people involved. Unlike Cino/Splita's split-at-checkout, the payer sends the first proposal (who was there, how much, or the scanned receipt) and every person with a share must accept. Itemized receipts become a claim board: claim, lock in, suggest edits.
    iii) Built for standing groups, not one-off dinners: roommates, couples, trips, teams. A budget per period with automatic top-ups up to each person's share, unlike one-off pools (Splitcard) or pay links (Split, splyt).
    iv) Trust with guardrails, which pooled accounts like Zeta lack: one open charge at a time; members who skip their top-up can only spend their own balance; negative balances are recovered from the member's personal card or the member is suspended; withdrawals locked while a split is pending; unresolved splits freeze after 7 days.
    v) Fair math: exact cents, tax and tip proportional to each person's items, shared items supported.

  3) why we have a chance
    i) US gap: the closest card products (Cino, Splita) are Europe/UK, and Split Mobile's card is still on its roadmap.
    ii) Recurring household spending (groceries, utilities, rent extras, trips) is where IOU apps create the most chasing, and where a standing fund makes the most sense.
    iii) Existing apps' top complaints (scan accuracy, fees on repayment, slow payouts, one person carrying the bill) are what our flow removes: scans are checked against the card charge and can be fixed before claiming, and there's no repayment step at all.
    iv) The rules engine is already built and tested; the same code would approve real card taps.

  4) honest weaknesses (judges may ask)
    i) Regulation: pooled consumer money needs a partner bank and an issuer that supports consumer programs (launch.txt 0.ii). Cino has licensed partners and funding; we don't yet.
    ii) Pre-funding friction: members put money in before spending, unlike pay-later IOU apps.
    iii) Agreeing after the purchase can stall; the 7-day freeze and first-proposal rule limit that, but it's a real cost.
    iv) Network effects: Splitwise and Venmo are already on everyone's phone.

  5) one-line pitch
    "A shared fund with a card for everyone: spend together, agree on the split after, and nobody ever fronts the bill or chases a friend for money."
